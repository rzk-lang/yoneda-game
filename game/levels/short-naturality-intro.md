This chapter proves naturality again, by a shorter route. It is an **alternative** to "Naturality of representable transformations", not a continuation: both reach the same theorem, and you need only one of them.

The difference is where the work goes. The longer route builds the square's boundary explicitly, names its diagonal, and proves a coherence for each half. This one proves a single lemma about an arbitrary square, with no boundary data named at all, and then feeds every later square to it whole. Four levels instead of nine, at the cost of one abstraction up front.

The route follows an exercise sheet Emily Riehl prepared for students. Its levels are marked as extras, so nothing here is required to finish the game.

*By the end you will be able to:* turn any square in a Segal type into an equality of composites, and use that to get naturality in three further steps.
